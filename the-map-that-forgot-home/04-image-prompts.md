# Image Prompts: Every Picture the Book Needs

*These are prompts for an illustrator or an image-generation tool, not finished art. Character looks follow the supplied character sheet.*

**Decisions applied:**
- The hometown is **Wrenmouth**.
- The map follows the **story geography** in the development package (Bellbridge joins Lantern Quay to the central island; the Night Market moves).
- **Tavi has no animal companion**, so leave it out of all art.
- The sheet's fantasy creatures are not used.

---

## A. House style (paste at the start of every interior prompt)

> Black-and-white children's book illustration, confident ink line with soft crosshatching and stippled tone, high contrast and print-friendly, warm and adventurous rather than scary, middle-grade (ages 8–12), clean white margins, no text unless stated, consistent character designs.

Cover and character-reference sheets may be in full colour: painterly storybook style, warm lamplight against cool silver-blue sea.

## B. Character reference sheets (make these first so every later image matches)

For each character, make one sheet: front view, side view, back view and three expressions. Use a plain background and full body.

**1. Mara Ellery (10, the viewpoint character).** Small, slim girl with shoulder-length wavy chestnut-brown hair, freckles and bright, watchful eyes. Tan canvas jacket over a cream top, dark trousers, sturdy brown boots, brown backpack. Always holding a spiral sketchbook with a pencil, often with a second pencil behind her ear. Expressions: concentrating (slight frown, "her grandad's frown"), worried, quietly determined.

**2. Idris Bello (11).** Tall, long-armed British-Nigerian boy with short, dark, tightly curled hair. Brass goggles pushed up on his forehead, blue work overalls with braces over a rolled-sleeve shirt, a canvas tool roll strapped across his chest and a spanner in one hand. From Chapter 2 on, a coil of bright blue rope over one shoulder. Expressions: proud grin, frustrated, sheepish.

**3. Bea Nowak (10).** Slight girl with long, straight black hair and a small grey gull feather tucked behind one ear; dark, calm eyes. Brown jacket with a satchel strap across it, binoculars round her neck (missing from Chapter 17 on), small bird notebook, a battered flapjack tin. Expressions: quietly observant, shy, and (late in the book) shouting with confidence.

**4. Tavi Sallow (11, from the Fold).** Wiry boy with curly copper-ginger hair under a slouchy blue cap, lots of freckles and a huge grin. Chunky brown scarf wrapped high, with a smudge of soot on one cheek. Carries a long wooden lamplighter's hook-pole with a small tin echo lantern hanging from the hook. Expressions: cheeky grin, bluffing, frightened (staring at open water).

**5. Ro Sallow, Head Lamplighter.** Tall woman in her sixties with cropped grey hair, ropy forearms and a tired, kind, weathered face. Leather apron, rolled sleeves, a wick-trimmer on her belt.

**6. The Surveyor (Ansel Crane).** About 70; tall and stooped, with silver hair combed neatly and round spectacles on a fine chain. Ink-stained fingertips. A long, immaculate dark-grey coat with many small pockets. A precise, gentle face that is sad rather than cruel. *(Use only from Chapter 22; earlier he appears only as a silhouette.)*

**7. Nan (Dorothy "Dot" Ellery, 78).** Small, soft-faced older woman with white hair half-escaping its pins. Good blue wool coat over a cardigan, pink slippers, handbag on her knee.

**8. Dad (Tom Ellery).** Broad, friendly man in his forties, with sleeves pushed up, a white apron and a face shiny from the fryers.

**9. Edie Marrow (young, for a memory or photo).** A girl of about 18 in a 1960s dance dress, dark bobbed hair, a mischievous smile and ink on her fingers.

**10. Grandma Tull.** A tiny, very old toll-keeper with bright eyes, a shawl and fingerless gloves, sitting at a tollhouse window.

**11. Orrin Pell (19, Chainman).** Lanky young man in a long grey coat with brass measuring-chain links clipped to his belt. An earnest, uncertain face.

**12. Moss Haverly.** An elderly beekeeper of the Canopy with a gauze veil pushed back and a slate board on a string round the neck.

**13. Amma Oduya.** Very old, upright woman with a wrapped headscarf, many bracelets, a stick carved like a branch and a fruit-picker's basket on her hip.

**14. Captain Wim.** Big, bearded boat trader in an oilskin hat, with a pipe (unlit) and crates of fruit.

**15. Saskia and Joss Sallow.** Tavi's parents. Saskia is fierce and straight-backed, with ginger hair like Tavi's in a braid and a ring of keys at her belt. Joss is a gentle rope-maker with coils of rope over both shoulders.

**16. Ezra and Pim.** A marsh elder in reed-woven waders and his seven-year-old grandchild with muddy knees and a big gap-toothed smile.

**17. Sorrel Penn.** A grumpy old hermit with a wild beard and a faded grey Chainman's coat patched at the elbows. Binoculars and birdseed in his pockets.

## C. Place references (for consistency; can double as chapter-opening vignettes)

**P1. Wrenmouth.** A small British seaside town: a curved harbour wall with fishing boats, an orange lifeboat station, a steep street (Fore Street) of terraced houses, the stubby church tower of St Wenna's, and a fish-and-chip shop called Ellery's on the harbour front. A white lighthouse on a grassy headland (Gull Head). An old stone river bridge inland.

**P2. Gull Head lamp room.** Round room at the top of the lighthouse. A huge beehive-shaped glass lens in brass framing in the centre, a curved wooden chart table with wide flat drawers around the walls, a small rusty door out to the gallery, and a clockwork mechanism beneath the lens.

**P3. The Lamp Road.** A narrow, pavement-wide road made of golden light stretching across a flat, silver, misty sea under a plum-coloured sky, with a lighthouse behind and a lamp-filled harbour ahead.

**P4. Lantern Quay.** A rocky island harbour of stone quays and tall narrow houses with lamps everywhere: on poles, strung between houses, in every window, on every boat. Lamplighters on ladders and stilts, and a tall Great Lamp tower at the end of the harbour wall glowing gold.

**P5. Bellbridge.** A long, high, arched stone bridge across the silver Slack, lined on both sides with small houses, washing lines and window boxes. Twenty-six bells of different sizes hang in little frames along its length, their ropes all running back to a tollhouse at the western gate.

**P6. The Whispering Canopy.** A dense, ancient forest of broad trees with huge rustling leaves, dim green light, rope walkways and platforms high in the branches, and beehives in hollow trunks. Faint curling lines in the air suggest sound bending.

**P7. The Upside-Down Orchard.** A rocky crag floating in the air above the Slack. Orchard trees grow on top, and also hang from its underside with their roots in the rock and branches pointing down, heavy with fruit. Pickers work beneath in rope nets, standing upside down. A cable and basket ferry (the Ropewalk) runs to it from the forest.

**P8. Glasswater Marsh.** Wide, perfectly still mirror-pools between beds of pale, glassy reeds that chime faintly. Herons wade, water-boatmen skate, and there are woven reed-mat homes on the floating isle of Heron Holm. The reflections are confusingly perfect.

**P9. The Night Market.** Dozens of boats lashed together into floating lantern-lit streets: dumpling stalls, rope stalls, hammocks strung between masts, and bunting made of fabric scraps. Gangplanks join deck to deck over dark silver water.

**P10. The Flooded Observatory.** A grand stone observatory with a great cracked dome, half-sunk in a lagoon. Water laps at its upper windows; one dry window glows. Inside: a star-chart painted round the dome wall and an old counterweighted float-lift.

**P11. The Surveyor's Tower.** A tall, thin, elegant tower on a sheer crag at the eastern edge of the Fold, backed by a wall of mist (the Rim). There's a domed top with a long telescope (the Long Glass) and a gallery round the map room. An iron chain-patterned bridge (the Chainway) leads to it. *Never show anything at the cliff's foot.*

**P12. The Rim.** A soft, towering wall of white mist at the edge of the silver sea, curling back on itself like a breaking wave in slow motion.

## D. Book images in order

### 1. Front cover (colour)
> Painterly middle-grade adventure cover. Four children stand on a narrow road of golden light stretching across a flat silver, misty sea at dusk: Mara (wavy chestnut hair, tan jacket, sketchbook) in front holding open an old map that glows; Idris (goggles, blue overalls, blue rope); Bea (long black hair, feather, binoculars); and Tavi (ginger curls, blue cap, brown scarf, lantern on a pole). Behind them a white lighthouse beams on a headland, a single gull frozen mid-air. Ahead, a harbour full of lamps, floating islands and, far off, a thin tower against a wall of mist. Plum-and-gold sky. Leave clear space at the top for the title "The Map That Forgot Home".

### 2. Front map (two-page spread)
Use the full prompt in `01-development-package.md`, section 5. **Required:** the nine tiny unlabelled dots between the Orchard and the Tower; a frame of 72 tick marks; Wrenmouth as an inset.

### 3. Interior illustrations (12)

**I-1. Chapter 1: The chart drawer.** [House style] The round lamp room of a lighthouse. Mara leans over a curved wooden chart table, map spread out, with a spool of thread and an old fountain pen beside it. On the map, islands are half-drawn, and a fine line seems to be inking itself outward from a tiny lighthouse. Behind her rises a huge beehive-shaped glass lens; Idris's legs and boots stick out from inside its brass frame, a spanner on the floor. Afternoon light through the windows. *No readable map labels.*

**I-2. Chapter 2: Nan on the harbour steps.** [House style] An old woman in a good wool coat and pink slippers sits on the top step of a harbour wall, tilting an old map to catch the light. Mara sits close against her shoulder, reading. Fishing boats behind. In the far distance, a single thick bank of fog sits exactly on an old stone bridge while the rest of the valley is clear. Tender and quiet.

**I-3. Chapter 3: Walking on light.** [House style] From behind and slightly above: three children walk single file along a narrow road of light across a flat, misty sea. Mara looks at the map, Idris looks down nervously, and Bea glances back. Ahead, a harbour crowded with lamps. Behind, a lighthouse on a headland with one gull hanging perfectly still in the air above it.

**I-4. Chapter 5: The toll token.** [House style] Close-up at a tollhouse window on a bell-lined bridge. Grandma Tull's gloved hand passes a small metal token to Bea while a lanky Chainman in a grey coat stands just behind, watching. On the wall, a wooden board is **clearly lettered** with: *1 Anchor, 2 Barley, 3 Candle, 4 Drum, 5 Ember, 6 Fern, 7 Gull, 8 Heron, 9 Iron, 10 Juniper, 11 Kettle, 12 Lark, 13 Moon, 14 Nettle, 15 Oar, 16 Pebble, 17 Quill, 18 Rope, 19 Salt, 20 Tide, 21 Urchin, 22 Vane, 23 Wren, 24 Xylophone, 25 Yarrow, 26 Zephyr.* This is the fixed list; Chapter 5 uses exactly these names.

**I-5. Chapter 6: Follow the bees.** [House style] A dim, ancient forest. Tavi and Idris argue, both pointing the wrong way towards curling lines of distorted sound. Bea stands slightly apart, pointing along a straight, dotted line of bees flying between the trunks. Mara is between them, looking from one to the other.

**I-6. Chapter 8: The Ropewalk.** [House style] A wicker basket hangs from a cable high above a misty silver sea. Idris winds glowing silver thread round a brake lever while Mara grips the side and Tavi stares at the drop, terrified. Bea points ahead. Beyond them, a floating rocky crag with orchard trees on top and more trees hanging upside down beneath it.

**I-7. Chapter 9: The underside.** [House style] Beneath a floating crag: Mara stands upside down on the rock among hanging fruit trees, hair falling "up" towards the sky below her, laughing in astonishment. Pickers in rope nets reach for fruit. Far below, in the silver water, a faint scatter of a few old posts (small, unremarked, not the focus).

**I-8. Chapter 11: The Night Market.** [House style] Boats lashed into a floating street at night, lanterns everywhere, a dumpling stall steaming, hammocks between masts. In the foreground, Tavi stands facing a fierce woman with a ginger braid and a ring of keys at her belt, cap in his hands. The other three watch from behind a stack of crates.

**I-9. Chapter 12: The last toll.** [House style] Seen from a reedy marsh: in the distance, a long bridge lined with houses fades into mist. Its middle span is already gone, and bells tumble into the water. In the foreground, four children in a small round coracle with a rescued old man and a small child, all watching in silence.

**I-10. Chapter 15: Behind the stars.** [House style] Inside a half-flooded observatory dome by torchlight. Idris hauls on the rope of a counterweighted float-lift. Bea holds the torch on the waterline, timing it. Mara reaches up to press one painted star on a wall covered in a star chart that, looked at closely, resembles a map of islands. Water ripples.

**I-11. Chapter 18: Low breath.** [House style] A cramped reed hide in grey morning light. Four exhausted children sit in a circle, sharing the last pieces of flapjack from a tin. The folded map lies in the middle of them, and nobody is holding it. Quiet, tender, the lowest moment.

**I-12. Chapter 22: The Great Chart.** [House style] A vast circular map room seen from the doorway. A huge map covers a round table, its border almost a complete line. Tall windows open to a gallery. The Surveyor is a tall silhouette at the window with a pen in his hand. Small in the foreground, Mara holds a torch. **Do not show the cliff's foot, any rope or any posts.**

### 4. Spot illustration
**S-1. Chapter 24: Pencil map.** [House style] A kitchen table in the evening. Mara and Nan, heads together, draw a map of Wrenmouth in pencil. Nan points at something, and the pencil circle round the harbour has a deliberate little gap in it. A small tin lantern sits on the table.

### 5. Back matter
**B-1. Crew Equipment page.** [House style] Field-guide layout of six small drawings, each in a box with space for a caption: the living map (folded, glowing lines, a torn corner); the echo lantern (small tin lantern with a candle marked in six notches); the spool of silver repair thread with a luggage label; the old fountain pen; a big chunky torch; a coil of blue rope. Below them, a row of smaller items: spiral sketchbook, pencils, ruler, binoculars, flapjack tin, water bottle, tool roll and spanner.

**B-2. Bell token puzzle panel.** [House style] A close-up of a stamped metal token with dots between numbers, beside a small sketch of the bell toll board. Leave space for the puzzle text.

**B-3. Design your own island.** [House style] A mostly blank page of stippled silver sea with a mist border and a small compass rose in one corner. One empty island outline floats in the middle, with a little dotted line leading off the edge of the page. Leave lots of white space for children to draw.
